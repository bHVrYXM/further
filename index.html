Where does your money go furthest right now?

    How this works

    Price level for each country = World Bank PPP conversion factor (local currency per international dollar) ÷ market exchange rate (local currency per US dollar). That gives prices relative to the US; dividing by your home country's level gives prices relative to home.

    “2.5× as far” and “100 from home is worth 250 there” mean the same thing: after exchanging 100 at today's rate, you can buy household goods and services there that would cost 250 at home.

    Which PPP: household final consumption PPP (PA.NUS.PRVT.PP), the closest match to what travellers buy. If a country's GDP PPP (PA.NUS.PPP) is more recent, that is used instead and the card says so.

    Which exchange rate: today's ECB reference rate via Frankfurter where the ECB quotes the currency, or where it is fixed to one the ECB quotes (e.g. CFA franc, Gulf pegs). Everything else uses the World Bank's annual average for the PPP year and is labelled Annual rate.

    Caveats: PPP figures describe the PPP year. If a currency fell since then, local prices have usually risen too, which PPP does not yet show — such countries look cheaper than they are. Cards flag a currency move of 10% or more. Prices are national averages: capitals and tourist areas cost more.

    Accuracy: every PPP factor shown is the World Bank value, rounded only for display (tolerance ±0.01%, checked by scripts/verify.mjs against the live API). Price levels are recomputed independently from the raw API values and must agree within ±0.1%.
